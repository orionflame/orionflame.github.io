01 · Geometric transformationsCore technique

Match animated geometry without surrendering control

Build a robust transform extraction and matching tool entirely in VEX. Orient geometry to a reference, even when an object is simulated near the origin and later restored to its animated world-space placement. Selectively match translation, rotation or scale, including nonuniform scaling, and invert the corresponding transforms when needed.

Comparable fitting accuracy to stock approaches, with finer control over the workflow and transform components rather than a claim of universally better numerical accuracy.

Every lesson is carefully scripted and edited for clarity. Topics are introduced with live concept sketches, then developed step by step in Houdini so you can understand the idea before applying it.

The drawings, code and viewport work stay visible in the recording. High-resolution instruction supports that detail; the explanation is what gives it purpose.

Volume 2 · Practical scenario

Take a velocity field apart
Control what you put back

A flow can look convincing while containing unwanted expansion, excessive spin or stretching in the wrong direction. Learn to see what is happening inside it—and target the behaviour you want to change.

Use velocity-gradient tensors to identify local stretching, compression and rotation. Explore Helmholtz decomposition to understand divergence-free, curl-free and harmonic contributions, with the role of boundaries made explicit.

Instead of scaling the entire velocity field, decide what to change. Directly editing component vector fields differs from prescribing new curl or divergence: the latter may require a constrained numerical reconstruction to obtain a consistent result.

This connects local flow analysis to reconstruction and linear solvers, extending the toolkit toward more deliberate operators for FLIP, Pyro and other fields. Solver-dependent reconstruction demonstrations are in development.

Volume 1 · Inside the lessons

The work behind each lesson

Planned before recording

Pragmatic VEX is a carefully structured course where every single topic, concept and idea is planned in advance with no idle moments, dead space, gratuitous talking or unplanned debugging for what should have been done offline.

Time spent learning

Almost all cooking and idle time is edited out. Drawing, code writing, viewport and network navigation, and node layout are sped up by 2–5× where appropriate.

The focus is the useful material in each lesson, rather than stretching the running time. Volume 1 contains 163 lessons and more than 15 hours of instruction.

Volume 1 · Depth and progression

Start beyond the basics

Build on the VEX you already know

The course assumes basic Houdini and VEX knowledge. It does not spend an hour explaining strings or how to call a function: introductory resources already cover that ground.

That leaves room for the advanced implementations shown here, including constructing Catmull-Clark subdivision in VEX.

If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, the lessons point you to the relevant Houdini documentation. Foundational references support the implementation without turning every advanced lesson into another introduction.

Ideas develop across lessons

The topics follow a thematic progression, building in complexity and intricacy as the course develops. Practical production tips are woven throughout the implementations.
